# Two of Us — Performance & Responsiveness Plan

**Status**: research complete, nothing implemented (2026-11-04)
**Goal**: every interaction feels instant — cold launch, resume, tap-to-confirmation when logging, timeline scroll, tab switch, sheet open, widget / Live Activity / watch freshness, and co-parent sync within ~10 s.
**Scope**: the whole app — phone app, widget / intent / notification-content extensions, watch app and complications, and the Brightwheel / SNOO / geofence integrations.

This document is the output of a static research pass, not a profiling session. Every finding below was located in the code, cross-checked by an independent reviewer against three questions (does the code really do this, would a parent notice it at today's data sizes, and is the proposed fix safe against the invariants documented in the comments), and kept only when it survived all three. Nothing was built or measured on a device. **Phase 0 (measure) comes first precisely because the magnitudes here are estimates.**

How it was produced: 13 single-lens sweeps (launch, foreground, Home render, timeline scroll, write latency, SwiftData, sync, widgets / intents, secondary screens, watch, integrations, build / runtime, perception, and a cross-cutting main-thread audit) plus a completeness-critic gap round produced 302 raw findings. Coarse de-duplication and adversarial review left **144 confirmed**; 40 were refuted as negligible or misread and 61 folded into a better-stated duplicate. Reviewers frequently adjusted a proposed fix to a safer variant; the appendix carries the adjusted version.

Data-size assumptions used throughout: Miller is ~4–5 months old; ~25 parent-logged events a day plus ~15 daycare-imported events per weekday since 2026-10-12. That is roughly 4,000–5,000 live rows today (plus soft-deleted edit rows), ~10,000 at one year, ~20,000 at two. Most costs below scale linearly with those numbers, so what is a hitch today is a stall next summer.

## 1. Diagnosis — six structural causes

Almost all 144 findings reduce to six patterns. Fixing the pattern fixes the findings; fixing findings one at a time does not fix the pattern.

**A. Home re-derives the whole history every second.** `HomeView.swift:114` wraps the hero card, log tiles, sleep card, SNOO cards and the tonight / up-next card in `TimelineView(.periodic(from: .now, by: 1))`. Everything the closure reaches is recomputed 60 times a minute on the main thread: `todaySummary` (a `StatsEngine.dailySummaries(days: 1)` pass over every feed, sleep and diaper ever logged) is evaluated four times per tick; `todayMarks`, `daycareToday` (twelve full passes), `firstDaycareEventToday`, `lastDaycareEventToday`, `sleepTarget` (×3), `activeSleep` (×5, each a full scan when no sleep is running), the four full-history maps behind `feedHint` / `predictedOz`, `NightSchedule` (×2, always materialising `occurrences()` even at noon, with comparators that allocate two UUID strings per comparison), and `ScheduleEngine` (×2). Nothing it renders changes faster than once a minute. Reviewers put this at tens of milliseconds every second on the core screen, competing with scrolling, the glow animation and sheet presentation, and growing with history. *(home-render-tick-1hz-minute-granularity, build-runtime-home-tick-full-scans, home-render-today-summary-x4, home-render-night-schedule-per-tick, and ten more.)*

**B. Unbounded `@Query` arrays across four resident tab roots, re-fetched on every save — twice.** `MainTabView` mounts Home, Schedule, History and Stats eagerly; once visited, every tab stays resident. Between them they hold 34 `@Query` properties, 21 of them whole-event-table fetches with only a `deletedAt == nil` predicate and no limit (Home alone has 17; nine serve daycare types read only for "today"). Every `ModelContext.save()` re-runs all of them and SwiftUI diffs ~12k managed objects, each dragging its `ckSystemFields` archive. And every local log saves **twice**: once for the insert, and again ~1–3 s later when CloudKit acknowledges the upload and `persistSystemFields` writes the change tag onto the same row (`SyncManager.swift:1113`, `:1205`), so the whole storm replays while the parent is reading the toast. There is no `#Index` anywhere in the schema, so the sorts on `timestamp` are full scans + sorts each time, and `RecordMapping`'s comment claiming "an indexed fetchLimit-1 lookup" describes an index that does not exist. *(launch-home-queries-full-tables-first-frame, gap1-resident-roots-21-unbounded-fetches, gap1-ack-save-second-storm, gap1-home-nine-daycare-tables-for-today, sync-no-index-on-model-id, swiftdata-no-indexes, secondary-screens-unbounded-queries-schedule-and-night-settings, write-latency-feedsheet-full-history-query.)*

**C. The write path does everything before the parent gets feedback.** `FeedSheet.log()` calls `store.logFeed`, and only after it returns fires `Haptics.success()` and `dismiss()`. `EventStore.logFeed` (`EventStore.swift:139-144`) synchronously runs the save, the sync enqueue, a Live Activity prediction refresh (13 fetches plus two engine builds, even when no sleep is running), `WidgetCenter.reloadAllTimelines()`, a Spotlight reindex request, three `NotificationManager` refreshes, an alarm re-arm and an intent donation. Three of those call `QuickLogger.make()` — which builds a brand-new `ModelContainer` over the App Group store each time (schema reflection over 18 models, coordinator, SQLite open, metadata check) — in the app process, which already owns `AppModelContainer.shared`. Reviewers estimate 50–150 ms between the tap and the haptic with reminders on, every log, every day; the haptic itself uses an unprepared generator. The Brightwheel importer pays the whole chain once per imported event, ~15 times per daycare day. *(write-latency-sync-side-effect-pipeline, write-latency-haptic-after-pipeline, launch-quicklogger-make-reopens-container, sync-rearm-reminders-opens-fresh-containers, sync-daycare-import-per-event-side-effects, write-latency-haptics-no-prepare.)*

**D. Full-table work on the foreground path, and N+1 scans in sync.** `applicationDidBecomeActive` runs `autoPurgeGhostEvents()` synchronously, which fetches every row of all 13 event tables (blobs included) on every activation — including dismissing Control Center or a permission alert — before the first frame. The same sweep runs inside the sync apply whenever the co-parent starts a nap. Inbound sync applies on the main context with two or three unindexed by-id scans per record; outbound record builds probe up to 18 tables per id; every batch re-runs the Live Activity reconcile (~15 fetches) regardless of what it carried. *(launch-autopurge-full-table-sweep-every-foreground, swiftdata-foreground-full-table-sweep, sync-ghost-sweep-full-store-on-open-sleep, swiftdata-sync-apply-on-main-triple-lookup, swiftdata-outbound-18-table-probe, foreground-pipeline-fires-on-every-activation, gap4-per-batch-reconcile-ungated.)*

**E. Deliberate holds and serial waits at launch and resume.** The launch curtain blocks touch for at least 1.45 s (0.9 s minimum hold + 0.55 s dissolve) and up to 3.55 s on every cold launch, and at least 1.15 s on every resume after 30 minutes away; underneath, the launch fetch is started twice behind two serial `accountStatus` XPC calls, `ensureStarted()` runs five times, Home's `@State` singletons do two synchronous Keychain reads during the first body, and the reconcile / re-arm block opens up to five more containers. The curtain's documented purpose (overnight events must not shift the list under a thumb) is real; its cost is paid on every open whether or not anything is in flight. *(launch-curtain-hold-design, launch-redundant-ensure-started-and-double-fetch, launch-foreground-reconcile-before-first-frame, launch-cold-curtain-after-quick-relaunch, gap3-brightwheel-keychain-read-in-homeview-init.)*

**F. Extensions pay a container open per invocation, and reload too often or not at all.** The widget provider opens a new `ModelContainer` and runs ~12 fetches per timeline request, once per placed widget kind; `reloadAllTimelines()` is called from every write, every sync batch, every push (twice) and every foreground (twice) — roughly 100+ requests a day against WidgetKit's budget, after which iOS defers reloads and widgets go stale — while `ControlCenter.reloadControls` is never called, so the Control Center sleep toggle shows stale state after any change made elsewhere. The small / lock-screen widgets render a frozen "2m ago" string with only three timeline entries. In-process sleep intents still write through a throwaway container and park their sync ids for "next launch". A wrist-logged event reaches the phone only via a throttled silent push. *(widgets-intents-reload-all-budget-fanout, widgets-intents-provider-container-per-kind, gap2-control-center-never-reloaded-after-state-change, widgets-intents-small-widget-static-since-text, widgets-intents-app-process-intents-bypass-live-sync-engine, watch-no-reverse-doorbell-to-phone.)*

A seventh, narrower cause deserves its own line because it is the single largest *stall*: **the prediction walk-forward runs on the main thread.** `PredictionArbiter` caches per (feed count, day), so the first Home tick after every logged feed — and every co-parent or daycare feed that lands, and every Stats visit — re-runs `PredictionAccuracy.feedTimeReport` / `feedAmountReport`: hundreds of ridge-regression fits with an O(N) array copy and three `Calendar` calls per training row each. The code comment says "~10 ms"; at today's history reviewers estimate 0.3–1.5 s, landing on the confirmation frame right after "Log feed". *(home-render-arbiter-walkforward-main-thread, main-thread-audit-prediction-accuracy-walkforward-on-main, secondary-screens-stats-accuracy-walkforward-main-thread.)*

```swift
enum Perf {
    static let signposter = OSSignposter(subsystem: "com.taylorseale.twoofus", category: "perf")
    @inline(__always) static func interval<T>(_ name: StaticString, _ body: () throws -> T) rethrows -> T {
        let id = signposter.makeSignpostID()
        let state = signposter.beginInterval(name, id: id)
        defer { signposter.endInterval(name, state) }
        return try body()

}
```

## 4. Workstreams

Ordered by impact per effort. Each lists the findings it resolves (ids are the appendix keys), the design, what must not break, and how to test. The first two change how the app *feels* daily; the rest make the gains hold as the data grows. Where several workstreams touch the same lines (`AppDelegate.swift:53, :59, :72, :83-87, :97-129`), land them in workstream order — WS1 first, WS7 last — so each edit builds on the previous one.

### WS1 — Quick wins (one session, S effort, low risk, high impact)

Resolves 36 findings. None changes a user-visible behaviour except for the better; all are local edits.

1. **Tick once a minute.** `HomeView.swift:114`: `TimelineView(.periodic(from: .now, by: 1))` → `TimelineView(.everyMinute)`. Every value the closure renders is minute-granular (`TimeFormatting.elapsed` truncates to minutes; `Urgency` thresholds are hour-scale; the strip's now-dot moves a quarter point per minute). The overnight-honesty contract in the comments at `HomeView.swift:195-205` holds at minute resolution. ~60× less work for a one-line change. *(home-render-tick-1hz-minute-granularity)*
2. **Bind derived values once per evaluation.** In the same closure: `let summary = todaySummary`, `let active = activeSleeps.first`, `let target = sleepTarget`, `let feedTimes = feeds.map(\.timestamp)`, `let feedSamples = feeds.map { ($0.timestamp, $0.amountOz) }` and pass them down. `todaySummary` alone is evaluated four times per tick today. *(home-render-today-summary-x4, home-render-sleep-target-x3, home-render-feed-hint-four-maps)*
3. **A real active-sleep query, with limits.** Add `@Query(filter: #Predicate<SleepEvent> { $0.endedAt == nil && $0.deletedAt == nil }, sort: \.startedAt, order: .reverse) private var activeSleeps` and use `activeSleeps.first` everywhere `sleeps.first { $0.isActive }` appears (five reads per tick, each a full scan when no sleep is running). The sort matters: two open rows can coexist between duplicate sweeps and today's code picks the newest-started. Put `fetchLimit = 1` on `QuickLogger.activeSleep` and `EventStore.activeSleep` (the same shape `lastEndedSleep` already uses). *(home-render-active-sleep-scan-x5, gap2-activesleep-fetch-unbounded)*
4. **`prefix(while:)` instead of `filter` on the newest-first arrays.** `timelineEntries` (`HomeView.swift:1267`, 13 filters), `daycareToday` (`:447`), `firstDaycareEventToday` / `lastDaycareEventToday`, `todayDiaperSplit`. Bind `let entries = timelineEntries` once in the section builder — it is assembled twice per body today (`isEmpty` check + `ForEach`). For sleeps keep the explicit lookback (`startedAt >= dayStart - 36h`) so an overnight sleep still credits today, and keep active sleeps explicitly. *(home-render-timeline-entries-double-eval, timeline-scroll-window-filter-scans-full-history, home-render-daycare-today-filter-order, home-render-predicted-wake-per-tick)*
5. **Indexes.** Inside every synced `@Model` body: `#Index<FeedEvent>([\.id], [\.deletedAt, \.timestamp])` (and `[\.externalID]`, `[\.sourceRaw, \.timestamp]` on the daycare-imported types; `[\.deletedAt, \.startedAt]`, `[\.deletedAt, \.endedAt]` on `SleepEvent`; `[\.loggedByID]` where identity rewrites scan; `[\.slotID, \.dayKey, \.deletedAt]` on `PlanOverride`). Index addition is an additive change Core Data performs as in-place lightweight migration — no `VersionedSchema` bump, consistent with rule 10 in docs/CLOUDKIT_RISKS.md. Do **not** use `@Attribute(.unique)`: it turns inserts into upserts and changes the duplicate-id semantics `autoPurgeGhostEvents` and `RecordMapping.apply` rely on. The extensions open the same store with the same model files, so all targets ship together, and the first launch after the update pays a one-time index build — this item deserves its own two-phone TestFlight soak, so start it first. *(sync-no-index-on-model-id, swiftdata-no-indexes)*
6. **Stop opening containers in the app process.** Install a provider once in `AppDelegate.didFinishLaunching` — `QuickLogger.appContainer = { AppModelContainer.isEphemeralFallback ? nil : AppModelContainer.shared }` — and have `make()` return a logger over a fresh `ModelContext(container)` when it is set (nil → today's on-disk open, so a widget-started sleep is never written to a throwaway in-memory store); main-actor callers may take `mainContext`. Switch `NotificationManager` (`:161`, `:221`, `:305`, `:359`, `:555-569`, `:594`), `SlotAlarmManager:46`, `FeedAlarmManager:55`, `SyncManager:1354` and `CareEventCatalog.recent` to it. Two rules the reviewers insisted on: (a) a `ModelContext` must not cross a suspension point — `FeedAlarmManager.reschedule` reads `logger?.lastFeed?.id` *after* `await requestAuthorization()` and `SlotAlarmManager` reads `logger.babyName` after its await, so capture those values before the first `await`; (b) the sleep intents compile into the widget target (project.yml), so they keep calling `make()` and simply benefit from the provider being set in the app process. `QuickLogger.swift` must keep compiling into the widget, watch and notification-content targets unchanged. Bonus correctness: `make()` opens the store without `TwoOfUsMigrationPlan`, and when the on-disk store is quarantined `AppModelContainer.shared` is the in-memory fallback whereas `make()` would return nil. *(launch-quicklogger-make-reopens-container; the first step of sync-rearm-reminders-opens-fresh-containers)*
7. **Haptics.** Long-lived static generators per style plus `Haptics.prepare()`; call `prepare()` on touch-down of the confirm button / tile (`PressableTileStyle` on `isPressed`), not on sheet appear (the engine stays warm only a few seconds). `.sensoryFeedback(_:trigger:)` is the SwiftUI-native alternative where a state change is the trigger. Reorder the safe sites to haptic-first: `LogButtons` (`Haptics.tap(); action()`), `LoggedToast` undo, `HomeView.delete` (`Haptics.warning()` before `softDelete`). The sheets keep haptic-after-write because a refused log must not get a success haptic — WS2 makes the write return sooner. *(write-latency-haptics-no-prepare, part of write-latency-haptic-after-pipeline)*
8. **Nudge the sync engine after local writes — measure first.** In `enqueueSave` / `enqueueDelete`, after a successful `state.add` on a live engine, cancel-and-restart a `pendingSend` task that sleeps ~300–500 ms (so an edit's two ids or a daycare import ride one batch), re-resolves the live engine and calls `sendChanges()`. ## 6. Product decisions

**6.1 The launch curtain.** Today: ≥ 1.45 s cold, ≥ 1.15 s resume-after-30-min, up to 3.55 s, on every open, by design (`LaunchOverlay.swift:22-25`). The design intent — overnight events must not land under a thumb — is sound. Options: (a) keep as is; (b) keep the moment but shorten it (min ~0.45 s + 0.25 s dissolve, cap 1.2 s, taps allowed during the dissolve) — arithmetic: ~0.4 s first frame + 0.45 s hold + 0.25 s dissolve ≈ 1.1 s to first tap with no fetch pending, and the dissolve is tappable; (c) additionally skip the moment entirely when the store is warm and the app was active within 30 minutes (~0.65 s to first tap), and otherwise play (b). **Recommendation: (c)**, sequenced after the per-save storm is gone (Phases 3–4), because skipping the hold re-exposes the foreground fetch's apply under the thumb. It keeps the mark for the launches where a fetch can actually move the list and removes the wait from the launches where nothing can. Late-arriving rows need an insertion animation and a stable anchor either way.

**6.2 Tick cadence and live counters.** Minute ticks (WS1) keep the house strings and VoiceOver labels. A seconds ticker on the active sleep card is possible via `Text(timerInterval:)` but changes the wording ("0:43:12") and the docs at `DayTimelineView.swift:97-99` call a seconds ticker noise. **Recommendation: minute ticks, no seconds ticker.**

**6.3 Pull-to-refresh semantics.** The spinner currently holds until Brightwheel *and* both CloudKit engines return (uncapped, 60 s URLSession default). **Recommendation:** let both finish in the background, show the spinner for at most ~6–8 s, and let `@Query` land whatever arrives later.

**6.4 Photos.** Daycare photos are not cached locally yet (`mediaData` is nil everywhere); avatars are inline, downscaled JPEGs. The inline choice was made for SwiftData's mirroring container, which the app no longer uses. **Recommendation:** add `thumbnailURL` (additive) and the in-process thumbnail cache now; when the photo-cache pass is built, store downscaled bytes or use `.externalStorage`, and decide which with a Phase 0 measurement of blob I/O after the query bounding.

**6.5 Haptic timing in the sheets.** The success haptic must stay after the write (a refused log must not buzz). **Recommendation:** accept that, and make the write return within a few ms (WS2) so the distinction stops being perceptible.

**6.6 Post-feed prompts and toast choreography.** Starting the toast after the sheet is gone and the prompt after the toast is gone lengthens the sequence by a few hundred milliseconds but stops the prompt sliding over a live Undo. **Recommendation:** do it.

**6.7 Explicit `sendChanges()` after writes.** Unproven gain against the engine's own scheduling; it touches the engine-identity guard. **Recommendation:** implement behind the `sync.send` signpost and keep it only if the measured enqueue→sent interval drops.

## 7. Considered and rejected

Reviewers refuted 40 findings as not worth doing and folded 61 duplicates. The instructive rejections:

- **`Baby` inverse to-many relationships faulting on insert** — SwiftData maintains the inverse with a one-time id fetch per context and an O(1) insertion thereafter; no measurable cost.
- **The sync engine's state-file write per `.stateUpdate`** — a few KB of fetch tokens plus the pending list, atomic write, on a later main-actor turn; sub-millisecond.
- **`relinkOrphanEvents`' 13 fetches per batch** — `baby == nil` compiles to a foreign-key probe that Core Data indexes; cheap. "Unindexed" must be checked per column, which is why the `id` / `externalID` / `timestamp` indexes matter and this one does not. (Gating it is still in WS6 for tidiness.)
- **`CKContainer(identifier:)` per access** — a wrapper over a daemon connection, not a round trip; only `accountStatus()` is real. The geofence re-add and alarm churn findings that were *only* about awaited XPC inside a `Task` were refuted for the same reason: awaited XPC never blocks a frame. The surviving geofence finding is about `assuming: .unsatisfied` clearing state.
- **Micro-fetches over 1–3-row tables** (owner fetched thrice per log, unlimited `.first` on Baby / settings, the `loggerPhoto` dictionary, `SharedSettings.canonical` unarchiving, the slot-actions sheet's fetch) — do not scale with history; invisible beside container opens and XPC.
- **`Color(hex:)` parsing, `AppFont` metrics, `UserDefaults(suiteName:)` recreation, `dictionaryRepresentation()` in the drain, the asset-outbox directory listing** — microseconds.
- **Release build flags** — XcodeGen's defaults already give `-O` / whole-module for Release; nothing to change.
- **The celebration hold, the curtain's blur rasterisation, the `PhotoStack` shadows, onboarding blur cost on launch** — real but imperceptible, or one-time.
- **`Avatar` decoding on the watch, the watch's whole-list `TimelineView`, nested `TimelineView`s on the active-sleep row, Home ticking while another tab is selected** — negligible at those row counts or already fixed by the minute tick.
- **Inline media blobs riding every query** — latent: `mediaData` is empty today; it becomes real only when the photo-cache pass lands, which is why it is a design gate in WS5 rather than a current cost.
- **Moving the sheet haptic before the write** — unsafe (refused logs); addressed by making the write faster instead.
- **A parallel `activeSleepStartedAt` flag in App Group defaults for the Control Center provider** — would drift from the store on edit / delete / restore / sync / ghost sweep; the provider keeps reading the real store.
